Galley kitchens

Best for
Condos, duplexes and narrow rooms where every inch is working space.
What we build
One wall of tall storage, one of counter and cooking, light at the far end.
Plan around
About 42 inches clear between the runs, so doors and drawers open freely.

L-shaped kitchens

Best for
Bungalows where the kitchen can open to the dining room.
What we build
Cabinets along two walls, the wall to the dining room gone, room for a table.
Plan around
The corner: pull-out corner units instead of a cabinet you can't reach.
Where we see it
Culver City, Burbank, Glendale, Highland Park.

U-shaped kitchens

Best for
Ranch houses with a family room beside the kitchen.
What we build
Three runs of cabinets, one turned into a peninsula with seating.
Plan around
About five feet between opposite runs, so two people can cook at once.
Where we see it
Sherman Oaks, Encino, Studio City, Pasadena.

Island kitchens

Best for
Open-plan and hillside homes with room to walk all the way around.
What we build
An island with seating, storage on both faces and power where the code asks for it.
Plan around
Clearance on every side, and venting if the cooktop moves to the island.
Where we see it
Beverly Hills, Santa Monica, Silver Lake, Los Feliz.

Every trade, one contract

Layout and walls

Open the kitchen to the living room, move the sink under the window, fit an island where the room allows it. We frame, set beams and finish the walls flush and square.

Cabinets

Custom and semi-custom cabinets built to the room's real measurements: handleless matte fronts, wood veneers or lacquer, with soft-close hardware throughout.

Counters and surfaces

Quartz, porcelain slab, stone and terrazzo, with mitred waterfall edges and full-height backsplashes cut from the same slab.

Plumbing, power and light

New circuits for induction and integrated appliances, water and gas lines moved where the plan needs them, and LED lighting that meets California's Title 24 energy code.

Permits and inspections

We draw the plans, file with LADBS or your city's building department, and book every inspection through final sign-off.

How a remodel runs

  1. On the first visit, we measure the kitchen, open a few cabinets to look at the plumbing and wiring, and talk through what you want to change and roughly what it costs.

  2. Before demolition, you approve the drawings, the cabinets, counters, fixtures and appliances, and a written estimate. Then we file for permits and order materials.

  3. By the final walkthrough, the kitchen has passed its inspections, the punch list is closed and the dust is gone.

Do you handle the permits?

Yes. We prepare the plans, file with LADBS or your own city's building department (Pasadena, Glendale, Burbank, Santa Monica and Beverly Hills run their own), and meet the inspector at every visit.

How long does a kitchen remodel take?

Most kitchens take six to ten weeks of construction once permits and cabinets are in hand. Moving walls, gas or plumbing adds time. You get a written schedule before work starts.

Can we live at home during the work?

Most families do. We seal off the work area, keep a clean path through the house, and can set up a temporary sink and counter for the weeks without a kitchen.

Do you design the kitchen too?

Yes. We measure, draw the layout and help you choose cabinets, counters, appliances and lighting, so one company answers for the kitchen from plan to finish.

Do you work in condos and duplexes?

Yes, including the HOA approval paperwork, insurance certificates and the work-hour limits many buildings set.
